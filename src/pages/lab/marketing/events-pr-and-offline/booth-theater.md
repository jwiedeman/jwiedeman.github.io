# Booth Theater

Booth theater turns a trade show booth from a static display into a short, scheduled presentation. Instead of waiting for people to stop and read a banner, you run a live demo or mini-talk on a repeating schedule. Each show gathers an audience and hands interested people to your sales team.

## How it works

Attendees walk past hundreds of booths and decide quickly where to stop. A crowd draws more people: when a group is watching something, others stop to see what it is. Booth theater creates that crowd with a 5 to 10 minute presentation that makes one complete pitch and ends with a clear next step, usually a one-on-one conversation at the booth.

## How to do it

1. Script a 5 to 8 minute talk: the problem you solve, a live demo, one customer story, and a specific call to action.
2. Make each part understandable on its own so people arriving midway can follow.
3. Set aside part of the booth as a stage: a slightly raised area, a large screen visible from the aisle, a wireless mic and speaker, and standing room. Add seating if space allows.
4. Run the show every 30 to 45 minutes and post the schedule at the booth.
5. Announce showtimes in the event app, on social, and in pre-event outreach ("Live demo at Booth 412 at 2:00 PM").
6. Choose a presenter for speaking skill, not seniority, who can deliver the fifteenth show as well as the first.
7. At the end of each show, point people to stations for hands-on demos or meeting booking. Have staff ready to talk right away.

## What to measure

- **Attendance per show**: headcount at each presentation, totaled across the event.
- **Post-show conversion rate**: attendees who stay to talk or have their badge scanned, divided by attendance.
- **Qualified leads per event hour**: qualified leads divided by hours the booth was open, compared with past static booths.

## Best practices

- Open with something that grabs attention from the aisle: a question on screen, a sound, or a physical demo.
- Involve the audience with a live poll, a volunteer, or a demo using their input.
- End by offering something useful, such as a report, trial, or consultation, in exchange for contact details.
- Record one show to reuse as a sales video and social content.

## Common pitfalls

- Running too long. Over 10 minutes, the show floor audience drifts away.
- No fixed schedule. Ad hoc shows cannot be promoted, so nobody plans around them.
- Too few staff after each show. If 25 people watch and only 2 reps are free, most leads walk away.
