# Rapid Newsjacking

Newsjacking means watching for breaking news and trending topics, then quickly publishing content that connects your expertise to the story. When an industry event, policy change, or trend dominates the news, the brands that comment within hours get most of the attention and press mentions. Those that wait days get little.

## How it works

Journalists covering breaking news need expert sources right away. Social audiences engage most with topics they are already discussing. Newsjacking meets both needs while the story is live. The window is short, often hours. A useful comment posted quickly beats a perfect one posted late.

## How to do it

1. Set up alerts for industry keywords, competitors, regulation, and major trends using a news or social listening tool.
2. Assign one person to scan alerts and trending topics every morning and flag opportunities.
3. Prepare templates you can adapt in under 30 minutes: a comment on a regulation change, a data-backed post on industry news, a social thread on a trend.
4. Agree in advance which topics spokespeople can comment on without executive review. For sensitive topics, set up a two-person approval that can respond within an hour.
5. When you spot an opportunity, draft, approve, and publish within a few hours.
6. At the same time, send journalists covering the story a ready-to-use quote and offer your spokesperson for interviews.
7. Boost the best post with paid social while the topic is trending. Log reach, press pickups, traffic, and links.

## What to measure

- **Time to publish**: hours from the story breaking to your content going live.
- **Engagement vs. baseline**: engagement rate on newsjacking posts divided by your average engagement rate.
- **Media pickups**: number of outlets that use your quote, data, or spokesperson.

## Best practices

- Only comment where you have real expertise or a new angle.
- Lead with data or a clear, well-reasoned contrary view rather than repeating what others have said.
- Keep headshots, bios, and contact details for three to five spokespeople ready to send.
- Before acting, check four things: Is it relevant to our audience? Do we have something new to say? Can we respond in a few hours? Is there reputational risk?

## Common pitfalls

- Using tragedies or disasters for promotion. When in doubt, stay silent.
- Responding too late. If the window has passed, let it go.
- Forcing a connection. If the link to your brand needs more than one sentence to explain, skip it.
