# Guerrilla Activations

A guerrilla activation is an unexpected, low-budget public stunt that gets attention through surprise and creativity instead of paid media. The goal is a moment people want to photograph and talk about. A good activation is not random. It expresses one specific brand message.

## How it works

Guerrilla marketing works by breaking the normal pattern of someone's day. An installation in an odd place or an interactive piece on a busy street makes people stop and pull out their phones. Sharing is more likely when the moment is visual, easy to understand in one post, and clearly tied to the brand. Without that tie, it is just entertainment.

## How to do it

1. Write down the one idea you want people to remember, such as "our product is fast" or "fresh food for everyone."
2. Design the activation as a direct expression of that idea.
3. Choose a location with heavy foot traffic from your target audience, such as near an industry conference or in a busy shopping district.
4. Make it photogenic and self-explanatory. Show the brand name clearly but not loudly. Add a hashtag to track sharing.
5. Get permits and property permissions before you start.
6. Hire a photographer and videographer. Cut a short social video and a longer behind-the-scenes version.
7. Pitch the story to local media and relevant creators, share the footage on your channels with paid support, and reply to people who post about it.

## What to measure

- **Earned media**: number of articles and posts covering the activation, compared with the cost of equivalent paid media.
- **Social reach and engagement**: views, shares, and comments on activation content, plus hashtag use.
- **Brand recall**: share of surveyed people who saw it and can name the brand and the message.

## Best practices

- Make it explainable in one sentence.
- Keep participation optional and pleasant. Do not block commutes or invade personal space.
- Time it to a related event or season when journalists are already covering the topic.
- Have a plan for what happens if it rains, draws too big a crowd, or gets shut down.

## Common pitfalls

- A memorable stunt nobody links to your brand.
- Confusing controversy with creativity. Stunts that offend, scare, or mislead damage the brand.
- No documentation. Most of the reach comes from the footage, not the live audience.
