# PRable Data Reports

A PRable data report is original research, such as a survey, data analysis, or benchmark study, designed to produce findings that journalists will cover and others will cite. A strong report can earn press mentions and backlinks and keep drawing search traffic long after launch.

## How it works

Journalists and writers need original data to support their stories. If you publish credible research with useful or surprising findings, you become the source they cite and link to. Data carries more weight than opinion because it can be checked. The research does not need to be academic. It needs to be relevant to your audience, new enough to be news, and clear enough to quote.

## How to do it

1. Choose a topic your audience cares about that also has wider media interest. Prefer a specific question ("How many hours a week do [role] spend on [task]?") over a generic "state of the industry."
2. Pick a method: a survey through a panel provider, an anonymized analysis of your own product data, or a new analysis of public datasets. Your own data is the hardest for others to copy.
3. Before writing, pull out the five to ten most surprising or useful findings. These become your pitch angles and social posts.
4. Write the report with clear charts, an executive summary, and a methodology section.
5. Build a landing page that shows the key findings openly. Gate only the full download, if at all.
6. Make a social graphic for each key finding, with your brand name on it.
7. Pitch the report to a targeted list of journalists, leading with two or three findings. Consider giving one outlet an exclusive first look.
8. Publish a blog post on the findings and share one finding at a time on social over the following weeks.

## What to measure

- **Media placements**: publications that cover or cite the report within 90 days.
- **Linking domains**: unique domains that link to the report or its landing page.
- **Downloads and leads**: gated downloads and the contacts they produce.

## Best practices

- State sample size, collection method, dates, and limitations. Journalists will not cite research they cannot check.
- Design charts and stat cards that work on social and carry your brand.
- Release the report near a related event or news cycle.
- Repeat the report every year. Year-over-year changes become a news angle of their own.

## Common pitfalls

- Findings that only confirm what everyone already believes. You need a few genuinely surprising results.
- Expecting coverage without outreach. Reports need active pitching.
- Gating everything. If journalists cannot see the key data without a form, they will skip it.
