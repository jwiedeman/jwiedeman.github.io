# Skip & Pause Subscriptions

Skip and pause options let subscribers delay their next shipment or stop shipments for a while without canceling. Common cancellation reasons are "I have too much product" and "I need a break." If the only choices are "keep receiving" or "cancel," those customers cancel. A middle option keeps them.

## How it works

Most cancellations are not a rejection of the product. They are temporary needs: a vacation, a stockpile, a tight month. Skip and pause turn a cancellation into a hold. Payment details stay on file and the subscription stays open. Bringing back a paused subscriber is much easier than winning back one who canceled.

## How to do it

1. Add three one-click options to the subscriber portal: "Skip next order," "Pause for 30, 60, or 90 days," and "Change frequency."
2. When a customer clicks "Cancel," show these options first: "Would you like to skip your next order, pause, or get deliveries less often?"
3. Set limits, for example one or two skips per quarter and a maximum pause of 90 days.
4. Resume paused subscriptions automatically at the end of the pause. Email a reminder 7 days before the next charge.
5. Send a confirmation when a pause starts, stating the resume date.
6. Report which products, segments, and subscription ages skip or pause most. If many customers skip every other order, offer a longer default interval.

## What to measure

- **Cancellation save rate**: customers who clicked cancel but chose skip, pause, or a new frequency, divided by all who clicked cancel.
- **Pause-to-resume rate**: paused subscriptions that resume, divided by all pauses.
- **Voluntary churn rate**: monthly cancellations divided by active subscribers, before and after launch.

## Best practices

- Make skipping take fewer clicks than canceling.
- Offer "send less often" as well as pause.
- Ask why they are skipping (too much product, budget, travel, other) and use the answers.
- Confirm a skip with a short, neutral message: "Your next order is now scheduled for [date]."

## Common pitfalls

- Hiding skip and pause. Customers who cannot find them will cancel.
- Indefinite pauses. A pause with no end date is a cancellation. Always set a resume date and tell the customer.
- Counting paused subscribers as active. Report them separately so churn is not understated.
