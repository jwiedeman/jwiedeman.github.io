# Tracking Page Upsells

Customers often check their order status several times before a package arrives. If tracking links go to a carrier site, those visits are lost. A branded tracking page on your own domain shows shipment status and adds product recommendations, offers, and useful content.

## How it works

Right after an order, customers are interested and trust the brand enough to have just paid. They also return to check on the order. A branded tracking page keeps that traffic on your site and gives you room for cross-sells, referrals, and product education, alongside the status information they came for.

## How to do it

1. Replace carrier tracking links in shipping emails with a branded tracking page on your domain. Use a post-purchase platform or build your own.
2. Put the shipment status and tracking number at the top of the page.
3. Below it, show a few recommendations based on the items in the order ("Goes well with your [product]").
4. Add a short-term offer, for example "10% off your next order, valid for 7 days."
5. Add content related to the order: care instructions, a how-to guide, or a short welcome video.
6. Change the content by status: product education before shipping, recommendations in transit, and a review request and refill reminder after delivery.
7. A/B test recommendation placement, number of products, and offer size. Report clicks, add-to-carts, and orders from the tracking page.

## What to measure

- **Revenue per tracking page visit**: revenue attributed to the tracking page divided by its visits.
- **14-day repeat purchase rate**: customers who place a second order within 14 days, for tracking page visitors vs. non-visitors.
- **Visits per order**: tracking page visits divided by orders shipped.

## Best practices

- Lead with the order status. Customers came to check their package.
- Recommend products related to what they ordered, not generic best sellers.
- Add a referral prompt ("Give a friend 15% off").
- Rotate recommendations between visits so they do not go stale.

## Common pitfalls

- Turning the page into a store. If tracking information is hard to find, customers lose trust.
- Leaving carrier links in shipping emails. The branded page only works if emails point to it.
- Showing the same products on every visit.
