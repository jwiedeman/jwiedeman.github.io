# NPS-Timed Referral Asks

An NPS-timed referral ask shows a referral offer right after a customer gives a score of 9 or 10. The customer has just said they would recommend you, so this is the easiest moment to ask them to do it.

## How it works

Many satisfied customers say they would refer a friend, but far fewer actually do. The gap is often timing. A referral email sent days later arrives after the good feeling has faded. Asking at the moment of the high score turns stated intent into action.

## How to do it

1. Send an NPS survey at a meaningful moment, such as after onboarding, after a repeat purchase, or at a fixed tenure. Use in-app, email, or SMS.
2. Branch on the score. 9 to 10 (Promoters) get the referral ask. 7 to 8 (Passives) get "What would make us a 10?" 0 to 6 (Detractors) get support outreach.
3. Show the referral ask immediately after a Promoter submits, on the thank-you screen or in a follow-up sent within minutes.
4. Include a unique referral link, a pre-written message they can edit, and one-tap sharing by email, text, social, or copy link.
5. Offer a reward to both sides, for example a free month for the referrer and a free first month for the friend.
6. If a Promoter has not shared after about a week, send one reminder with a different sharing option.

## What to measure

- **Promoter-to-referrer rate**: Promoters who share a referral link, divided by all Promoters who saw the ask.
- **Referral completion rate**: shared links that lead to a signup or purchase, divided by links shared.
- **Referred customer value**: revenue from referred customers compared with customers from other channels over the same period.

## Best practices

- Remove every step you can: pre-fill the message, generate the link automatically, and offer several share options.
- Show Promoters their impact, for example "Three friends you referred are still customers."
- Tailor the suggested message to the feature or product the customer uses most.
- For email surveys, send the ask in the same thread so it reads as a natural follow-up.

## Common pitfalls

- Asking Passives or Detractors for referrals. They just told you they are not fully satisfied.
- Waiting more than a day to ask. The moment passes quickly.
- Offering a reward too small to justify the social effort of recommending you.
