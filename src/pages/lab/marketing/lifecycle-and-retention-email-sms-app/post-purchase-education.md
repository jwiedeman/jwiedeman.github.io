# Post-Purchase Education

Post-purchase education is a sequence of automated messages sent after a purchase to help the customer succeed with the product. It reduces returns and support tickets and sets up the next purchase.

## How it works

Customers who know how to use a product are more satisfied, less likely to return it, and more likely to buy again. This flow closes the gap between what the customer expected and what they experience. It sends the right information at the right time: what to expect at shipping, setup on delivery, and deeper tips a week later.

## How to do it

1. Email 1, when the order ships: explain what is in the box, how it is packed, and any assembly needed. Add a short unboxing video or graphic.
2. Email 2, on the expected delivery day: send a quick-start guide with the first three things to do. Use numbered steps with images and link to a full guide or video.
3. Email 3, three to five days after delivery: explain the most common beginner mistake and how to avoid it.
4. Email 4, seven to ten days after delivery: share an advanced tip, a lesser-known feature, or customer photos showing the product in use.
5. Email 5, two to three weeks after delivery: ask one feedback question. Route happy customers to review and referral flows and unhappy customers to support.

## What to measure

- **Return rate**: returns among customers who engaged with the flow compared with those who did not.
- **How-to support tickets**: setup and usage tickets per 100 orders, compared between the same two groups.
- **90-day repeat purchase rate**: customers who buy again within 90 days, split by flow engagement.

## Best practices

- Match content to the product bought. A skincare buyer needs a routine guide. A cookware buyer needs recipes.
- Use short video for anything hard to explain in text.
- Time sends from carrier tracking data, not the order date. A setup guide that arrives before the product causes confusion.
- Put a support link in every email.

## Common pitfalls

- Upselling in the first week. Keep the early messages purely helpful.
- Sending first-timer content to repeat customers. Segment by purchase history.
- Writing like a manual. Focus on results ("how to get the best results") rather than specifications.
