# VIP & Loyalty Tiers

A tiered loyalty program places customers into membership levels, usually three or four, where each level earns better rewards and recognition. Unlike a flat points program, tiers give customers a goal and a status to protect, which encourages them to concentrate their spending with you.

## How it works

Tiers work for two reasons. People are more motivated to finish a goal once they see progress toward it. People also value status that others do not have. Make the next tier visible and reachable, and each purchase becomes a step toward it. Tiers must differ in ways customers care about. A small extra discount is not enough.

## How to do it

1. Define three or four tiers using annual spend or points. Set thresholds from your real spend distribution, for example a free entry tier and a top tier that only your best customers reach.
2. Give each tier clearly different benefits, for example early sale access, then free shipping, then exclusive products, then a dedicated contact or invite-only events. Each upper tier needs at least one perk that is truly exclusive.
3. Show current tier and progress to the next tier on the account page, on order confirmations, and in marketing emails. Example: "You are $127 away from Gold."
4. Create separate campaign tracks for upper tiers, with earlier access and first looks at new products.
5. Require members to re-qualify each year to keep their tier.

## What to measure

- **Tier advancement rate**: members who move up at least one tier in a year, divided by all members.
- **Revenue share by tier**: revenue from each tier divided by total revenue.
- **Tier churn rate**: members in each tier who lapse or drop a tier, per year.

## Best practices

- Enroll customers in the entry tier automatically at first purchase.
- Add at least one experience-based perk per upper tier, such as events, early access, or personal service, not just discounts.
- Send a quarterly status email with progress, points, and what the next tier adds.
- Send a "welcome to your new tier" email with a reason to use the new benefits right away.

## Common pitfalls

- Too many tiers. Customers lose track of where they stand.
- A top tier that is too easy to reach. It stops feeling exclusive.
- Offering only discounts. That attracts deal-seekers rather than loyal customers.
