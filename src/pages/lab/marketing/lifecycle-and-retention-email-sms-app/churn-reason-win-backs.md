# Churn-Reason Win-Backs

Churn-reason win-backs group lapsed customers by why they left and send each group a message that addresses that reason. A discount does not fix a quality complaint, and a feature announcement means nothing to someone who left over price.

## How it works

Customers leave for identifiable reasons: price, product problems, missing features, a competitor, a change in their situation, or simply not using the product. If you record the reason at cancellation, you can build a separate win-back path for each one. The message changes from "please come back" to "we fixed the thing that made you leave."

## How to do it

1. Add a required single-choice question to the cancellation flow: "What is the main reason you are leaving?" Offer five or six specific options plus "Other." Save the answer as a profile field in your CRM or ESP.
2. Create a segment for each reason: Price, Product Quality, Missing Features, Switched to Competitor, Not Using It, and Other/Unknown. Fill gaps for non-responders using support tickets and survey history.
3. Write one win-back message per segment. Price: offer a cheaper plan or annual billing. Product or features: show what has changed since they left. Low usage: show what they never tried and offer setup help.
4. Send the first win-back about 30 days after churn. Follow up around 60 and 90 days with a different angle each time.
5. Send a final message around 90 to 120 days asking what would bring them back. Share the answers with the product team.

## What to measure

- **Win-back rate by reason**: customers who reactivate in each segment, divided by the segment size.
- **Reactivated customer value**: revenue from won-back customers over the next 12 months, minus the cost of any discount offered.
- **Second churn rate**: share of won-back customers who cancel again within 90 days.

## Best practices

- For customers who switched to a competitor, send an honest comparison. Say where you are better and where you are still catching up.
- Offer price-driven churners an annual plan rather than a monthly discount. It secures a longer commitment.
- For low-usage churners, offer a guided setup session. The issue was usually activation, not value.
- Use SMS alongside email for customers who opted in and have stopped opening email.

## Common pitfalls

- Sending the same "we miss you" message to everyone regardless of reason.
- Offering deep discounts to customers who did not leave over price. You lose margin and they leave again at full price.
- Stopping after one message. Plan three or four touches over about 90 days.
