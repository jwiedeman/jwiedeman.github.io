# Endowment Effect

The endowment effect is the bias where people value something more simply because they own it. A mug that seems worth $5 on the shelf feels worth more once it is yours. In marketing, once customers have used, customized, or invested time in your product, they value it more and dislike the idea of losing it. Free trials, freemium plans, and customization features all rely on this.

## How it works

The endowment effect comes from loss aversion. Once something feels like "mine," giving it up feels like a loss. This is why car dealers push test drives, why SaaS onboarding asks you to set things up your way, and why subscription routines are hard to cancel. Ownership can be psychological, not just legal. Even imagining owning something can trigger the effect.

## How to do it

1. Design trials that get users to invest: import data, customize dashboards, invite teammates, and build workflows.
2. Use possessive labels in the interface: "Your dashboard," "Your recommendations," "Your progress."
3. Let users create things inside the product, such as saved lists, configurations, and collections.
4. At the end of a trial or in upgrade prompts, show what they have built: "You've created 12 workflows and invited 3 teammates. Upgrade to keep everything."
5. For physical products, offer home trials, try-before-you-buy, and generous return windows.

## What to measure

- **Trial activation depth**: count ownership actions per trial user (data imports, customizations, invites).
- **Trial-to-paid rate by activation depth**: compare conversion for users with few versus many ownership actions.
- **Try-before-you-buy return rate**: compare returns from trial programs against standard purchases.

## Best practices

- Get users to personalize and create early in the trial, while interest is high.
- At conversion moments, show saved items, history, and settings so users see what they would lose.
- Use ownership language everywhere: "Your plan," "Your team's workspace," not "the account."

## Common pitfalls

- Making the trial so limited that users cannot invest in it.
- Forcing users to start over when they upgrade (new account, data migration), which throws away what they built.
- Counting on ownership alone. Users will still leave a customized product that does not solve their problem.
