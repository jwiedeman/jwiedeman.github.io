# Temporal Reframing

Temporal reframing means stating a price in a small time unit so it feels smaller. "$1 a day" feels cheaper than "$365 a year," even though the totals match. Daily, weekly, or per-use framing can make a large price feel manageable.

## How it works

How big a number feels depends on the scale it is shown in. A $29 monthly fee invites more thought than "about $1 a day," because the monthly figure reads as a budget decision and the daily one does not. The frame works best when the daily amount matches a small expense people already make without thinking, such as a cup of coffee. The cost does not change; only how it feels does.

## How to do it

1. Calculate your price per day, per week, and per use.
2. Pick the smallest unit that still sounds reasonable. Per-minute pricing feels manipulative.
3. Pair it with a familiar comparison: "$2.50 a day, less than a morning latte."
4. Use the frame on pricing pages, in emails, and in ads for subscriptions and recurring costs.
5. A/B test the reframed price against the standard display.
6. Reframe benefits the same way: "Saves your team 2 hours a day."

## What to measure

- **Conversion rate by price frame**: A/B test yearly, monthly, and daily price displays.
- **Price objection rate**: count how often prospects push back on price in sales calls.
- **Average contract value**: check that the daily frame is not steering buyers toward shorter plans.

## Best practices

- Use the daily frame where the annual price prompts budget scrutiny. For cheap products, it may not be needed.
- Compare the reframed price to a small, everyday expense such as coffee, a streaming plan, or parking.
- Always show the actual billed amount next to the reframed price.

## Common pitfalls

- Overdoing it with expert buyers. A B2B buyer knows "$3.29 a day" is about $1,200 a year and may resent the framing.
- Hiding the real billing amount. Buyers feel tricked when the invoice arrives.
- Using per-use pricing on one-time purchases when customers cannot picture using the product that often.
