# Hyperbolic Discounting

Hyperbolic discounting is the bias where people strongly prefer rewards now over larger rewards later. Someone may take $50 today over $100 in six months. In marketing, immediate and concrete benefits usually beat promises of future value, so offers that deliver something right away tend to convert better.

## How it works

Standard economic models assume people discount the future at a steady rate. In practice, the discount is steepest for short delays and flattens over longer ones. That is why "See results today" tends to beat "See results in 90 days," even when the later result is bigger. The immediate reward feels certain; the future one feels abstract. Deliver value early instead of fighting the bias.

## How to do it

1. Restructure the offer to deliver something immediately: instant digital access, a quick-start guide, or same-day onboarding.
2. Lead every message with the immediate benefit, then mention the long-term one. For example: "Get your first report in 5 minutes."
3. Give instant rewards for key actions: a discount for signing up today, a bonus unlocked on finishing onboarding.
4. Cut the time between purchase and the first useful result.
5. Fill long payoff periods with small rewards: weekly progress reports, early milestone badges, and interim results.

## What to measure

- **Time to first value**: measure time from signup or purchase to the first meaningful outcome.
- **First-week retention**: the percentage of new users still active after 7 days.
- **Instant-incentive lift**: A/B test an immediate reward against an equal delayed reward.

## Best practices

- Lead with the fastest win the product can deliver, then explain the long-term value.
- Offer a quick-start path that skips full setup and gets users to a result in minutes.
- Frame long commitments around something certain now: "Lock in this rate today" rather than "Save over the next year."

## Common pitfalls

- Requiring weeks of setup before users see any value.
- Promising instant results the product cannot deliver.
- Selling only on long-term ROI and losing to competitors who deliver value faster.
