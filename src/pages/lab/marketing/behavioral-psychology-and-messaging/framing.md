# Framing

Framing is the principle that how information is presented changes how people respond to it. "95% fat-free" and "contains 5% fat" describe the same product but feel different. Every headline, price display, and feature description is a framing choice.

## How it works

People judge information in context, not in a vacuum. Tversky and Kahneman showed this with their "Asian disease problem": the same outcomes described as lives saved or lives lost led people to opposite choices. For marketers, one benefit can be framed as a gain ("get more sign-ups"), as avoiding a loss ("stop losing sign-ups"), or as a social norm ("join other teams who get more sign-ups"). Each frame works differently for different audiences and funnel stages.

## How to do it

1. For each core benefit, write three versions: a gain frame, a loss frame, and a social frame.
2. A/B test the three versions.
3. Pick a price frame on purpose: show the monthly cost to make it feel small, or the annual savings to stress value.
4. Turn features into outcomes. Instead of "256-bit encryption," write "Your data is protected with bank-grade encryption."
5. Use comparisons to set the context, for example: "Less than the cost of a daily coffee."
6. Test positive and negative frames in email subject lines, ad headlines, and page headers.

## What to measure

- **Conversion rate by frame**: A/B test gain, loss, and social versions of the same page.
- **Message pull-through**: note which phrases prospects repeat back on sales calls.
- **Click-through rate by frame**: compare CTR across framings in email and ad tests.

## Best practices

- Match the frame to the audience. Loss frames tend to suit risk-averse buyers; gain frames tend to suit early adopters.
- Make complex decisions look simple. Two clear choices beat a large feature matrix.
- Use positive frames for brand building and loss or urgency frames near the bottom of the funnel.

## Common pitfalls

- Using technical terms the audience does not understand. "99.9% uptime" means little without saying what it means for them.
- Pushing negative frames so hard that they cause anxiety instead of action.
- Assuming one frame works for everyone. Test by segment and funnel stage.
