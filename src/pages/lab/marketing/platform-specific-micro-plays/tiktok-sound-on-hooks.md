# TikTok Sound-On Hooks

Most TikTok users watch with sound on, so the first sound in an ad matters as much as the first frame. That can be a spoken line, a trending sound, or a distinct audio cue. Ads built for sound usually fit TikTok better than muted creative copied from other platforms.

## How it works

TikTok grew out of a music app, and its culture runs on trending sounds, original audio, and voiceover. The audio hook is the first words spoken, the first beat, or the first sound effect. A direct opening line ("Nobody talks about this...") or a recognizable sound can stop a scroll as well as a strong visual. Designing for sound is the normal way to make content on TikTok.

## How to do it

1. Check trending sounds each week in TikTok Creative Center and the in-app sound library. Trends fade quickly, so use them while they are rising.
2. Write audio hooks for the first one or two seconds: a question, a contrarian statement, a casual first-person line ("OK, I have to tell you about this"), or a trending clip.
3. Test audio separately from visuals. Keep the same footage and swap in three to five different audio openings.
4. Tell the main story in voiceover. Use on-screen text to support the audio, not replace it.
5. Mix trending sounds with original audio. Trending sounds feel current; original voiceover or music is more distinctive.
6. Check that any music you use is cleared for ads, for example from TikTok's Commercial Music Library.

## What to measure

- **Average watch time**: from TikTok Ads Manager, per ad.
- **2-second and 6-second view rates**: 2-second and 6-second views divided by impressions.
- **Audio mentions**: comments that quote the voiceover or mention the sound.

## Best practices

- Start with the strongest audio moment. No slow musical intro or silence.
- Match the voice and pace to your audience.
- Build recognizable brand audio, such as a recurring sound or narrator.

## Common pitfalls

- Reusing text-heavy, muted ads from other platforms without reworking the audio.
- Using a trending sound after the trend has passed.
- Over-producing the audio so it sounds like a TV spot.
