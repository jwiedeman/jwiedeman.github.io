# LinkedIn Conversation Ads

LinkedIn Conversation Ads are sponsored messages delivered to a member's LinkedIn inbox. Each message has several response buttons, and each button leads to a different follow-up message or link. Prospects pick the path that fits them, which makes the ad self-qualifying.

## How it works

Conversation Ads pair LinkedIn targeting (job title, company size, industry, seniority) with a short, chat-style exchange. The message comes from a named person at your company and offers a few choices such as "Show me a demo", "Send the case study", or "Not right now". Each choice opens a different branch. Prospects get the content they asked for, and you learn what they care about from the button they press.

## How to do it

1. List two or three distinct needs your audience has, for example "see a demo", "read a case study", and "understand pricing". Each becomes a branch.
2. Choose a sender: a real team member whose role fits the message, such as a sales lead or subject expert.
3. Write the opening message in a plain, personal tone. Keep it short and give one clear reason for reaching out.
4. Add two or three buttons per message, each leading to a clearly different next step.
5. End every branch with a measurable action: a demo booking page, a case study download, a pricing page, or a Lead Gen Form.
6. Set up conversion tracking for each destination and review button clicks and conversions in Campaign Manager.

## What to measure

- **Open rate**: opens divided by sends, from Campaign Manager.
- **Click-through rate by button**: clicks on each button divided by opens. This shows which topic interests your audience most.
- **Cost per lead by branch**: spend divided by leads generated from each branch.

## Best practices

- Send from a real person with a profile photo, not a company page.
- Keep each message short and conversational. It should read like a chat, not a newsletter.
- Test different structures: "choose your interest", "answer a question", and "pick a resource".
- Target narrowly by job title, company size, and industry.

## Common pitfalls

- Writing the message like a formal marketing email.
- Building too many levels of branches, which feels like a phone menu.
- Targeting too broadly, which raises cost without improving lead quality.
