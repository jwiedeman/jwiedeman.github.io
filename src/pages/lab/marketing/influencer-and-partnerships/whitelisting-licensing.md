# Whitelisting & Licensing

Whitelisting, also called creator licensing, means running paid ads through a creator's social account with their written permission. The ad shows the creator's name and profile instead of your brand's. You get the creator's credibility plus the targeting and scale of paid media.

## How it works

A whitelisted ad shows the creator's photo, handle, and engagement. It looks like a normal post, not a brand ad, which often earns more attention. On Meta, this runs as partnership ads, where the creator grants your business access. On TikTok, it runs as Spark Ads, using an authorization code from the creator.

## How to do it

1. Write whitelisting into the creator contract. Name the platforms, the length of usage rights (for example, 30 to 90 days), and whether you may edit the content (captions, length, call to action).
2. On Meta, have the creator approve your business as a partner on their Instagram or Facebook account, then select their content when you build the ad.
3. On TikTok, ask the creator for a Spark Ads authorization code for the post, with an agreed duration. Enter it in TikTok Ads Manager.
4. Run the ads as ad-only posts so they do not clutter the creator's feed. Start with broad targeting. Test several creator assets at once.
5. Compare creator ads with your brand ads on cost per acquisition, click-through rate, and hook rate. Move budget to the winners and brief other creators in the same style.

## What to measure

- **Hook rate**: video views of 3 seconds or more divided by impressions.
- **Cost per acquisition**: ad spend divided by conversions, compared with brand-account ads.
- **Days to fatigue**: days until cost per acquisition rises above your target, which tells you when to rotate content.

## Best practices

- Secure usage rights longer than you expect to need. Renegotiating mid-campaign is slow and costly.
- Use ad-only posts rather than boosting organic posts, so you control targeting, bidding, and placement.
- Brief creators for raw footage with a strong opening line. Overproduced content loses the benefit.

## Common pitfalls

- No written permission. A creator can revoke access and stop your best ad.
- Adding heavy logos and text overlays until the ad looks like a brand ad.
- Leaving whitelisting fees out of the budget. Creators usually charge extra for paid usage of their identity.
