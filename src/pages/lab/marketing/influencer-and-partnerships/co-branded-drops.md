# Co-Branded Drops

A co-branded drop is a limited-edition product made by two brands, or a brand and a creator. Both promote it to their audiences at the same time. The partners share the cost and the risk.

## How it works

The best partner shares your customer's values but reaches people who do not know you yet. Each side introduces the other to its audience. A fixed quantity and a fixed release date give buyers a reason to decide now instead of later.

## How to do it

1. Pick a partner whose audience matches your target customer but who does not compete with you. Check their audience age, location, and interests, and confirm the brands fit together.
2. Design a product or bundle that makes sense for both audiences and could not exist without the collaboration.
3. Plan backward from the drop date. For example: product final 4 to 6 weeks out, content 3 to 4 weeks out, teasers 2 weeks out, early access for email and SMS 1 week out, public launch on drop day.
4. Launch together. Build one shared landing page, line up social posts, and give both email lists early access.
5. Make a fixed quantity and state the number publicly ("500 units") instead of a vague "limited edition."

## What to measure

- **Sell-through rate**: units sold divided by units made, checked at 24 and 48 hours.
- **New customer share**: first-time buyers divided by all buyers of the drop.
- **Earned mentions**: press pieces, social mentions, and creator posts about the drop.

## Best practices

- Sign the revenue split and cost split before product work starts.
- Share one content calendar so both teams post at agreed times.
- Plan the follow-up: restock news, behind-the-scenes content, and a survey about future collaborations.

## Common pitfalls

- Choosing a partner by follower count instead of audience fit.
- Making so few units that most interested buyers leave frustrated.
- Not collecting emails from people who missed the drop. Put them on a waitlist for the next one.
