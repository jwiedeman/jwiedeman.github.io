# Advertorial Pre-Sells

An advertorial pre-sell is an article-style page that sits between your ad and your product page. Instead of sending cold traffic straight to a sales page, you send it to a story that explains the problem and introduces the product. Visitors arrive at the product page with context and a reason to care.

## How it works

People who have never heard of you have no trust and no context. A product page asks them to decide before they know why it matters. An advertorial reads like a magazine article or blog post. It tells a relatable story, describes the problem, and presents the product as a solution. By the time readers click through, they understand the offer.

## How to do it

1. Write a headline that promises useful information, such as "How I finally fixed my back pain."
2. Tell a story that matches your customer's experience. Describe the problem in specific terms.
3. Use an editorial layout: body text, subheadings, pull quotes, images. Leave out site navigation and product promos in the first half of the page.
4. Add credibility inside the story: expert quotes, sources, and customer testimonials.
5. About two-thirds of the way through, introduce the product as what solved the problem. Add social proof and a link to the product page.
6. Test one variable at a time: story angle, headline style, or CTA format (text link, button, or product card).
7. Label the page as sponsored or advertising where platform rules or law require it.

## What to measure

- **Read-through rate**: Share of visitors who scroll at least 75% of the article.
- **Click-through to product page**: Share of advertorial visitors who click to the product page.
- **Full-funnel ROAS**: Revenue divided by ad spend across ad, advertorial, product page, and purchase. Compare it to sending the same ads straight to the product page.

## Best practices

- Write at a plain reading level. Keep paragraphs to two or three sentences.
- Match the ad creative to the article style. An ad that looks like a product promo sets the wrong expectation.
- Use "continue reading" or "see how it works" CTAs on the article. Close the sale on the product page.

## Common pitfalls

- Writing something so salesy it reads like a long ad. Readers who feel tricked leave and remember the brand.
- Making the article too long for the traffic source. Social traffic usually reads less than search or native traffic.
- Using only one CTA at the bottom. Add inline links so readers who stop early can still reach the product.
