# Multi-Step Forms

A multi-step form splits a long form into a sequence of short screens with a progress indicator. Instead of showing 15 fields at once, you show three or four at a time. The form looks easier to start, and each finished step makes the next one feel worth doing.

## How it works

When people see a long form, they estimate the effort and often leave before starting. A multi-step form shows only the first small step. Once someone enters a name and email, they have made a small commitment and are more likely to continue. The progress bar shows how much is left.

## How to do it

1. Group the fields into topics. For example: contact details, qualifying details, specific needs, review and submit.
2. Put the easiest fields first, usually name and email.
3. Move sensitive fields, such as phone number or budget, to later steps.
4. Show a progress bar or step count on every screen.
5. Validate each step before moving on, not all at the end.
6. Save partial answers at each step so you can follow up with people who stop.
7. Build it in a form tool with step support (Typeform, HubSpot, Gravity Forms) or in custom code. Test it on a phone.

## What to measure

- **Step completion rate**: Share of users who finish each step. A big drop shows which step needs work.
- **Overall completion rate**: Share of starters who submit the last step. Compare it to your old single-page form.
- **Partial lead recovery**: Share of people who stopped mid-form and later converted from follow-up.

## Best practices

- Keep each step to three to five fields.
- Use conditional logic to skip steps that do not apply. A person who picks "Individual" should not see company questions.
- Keep a back button available but visually quieter than "Next."

## Common pitfalls

- Splitting a short form into one field per step. Multi-step forms help when the form is long, roughly eight fields or more.
- Not saving partial answers. A name and email from an unfinished form is still a lead.
- Putting the hardest questions first. Start easy and ask for more later.
