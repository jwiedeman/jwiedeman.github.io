# Post-Purchase Upsells

A post-purchase upsell is an offer shown right after payment, usually on the order confirmation page. The customer can add an item to the order with one click, without re-entering payment details. Because the first sale is already complete, the offer cannot cause cart abandonment.

## How it works

Right after buying, customers are engaged and have just trusted you with their money. A relevant offer at this moment is easy to accept. The worst outcome is "no thanks," and the original order is unaffected.

## How to do it

1. Install a post-purchase tool for your platform (for example ReConvert or CartHook on Shopify), or build a custom confirmation-page offer.
2. Charge the stored payment method when the customer clicks "Add to order." Ask for nothing else.
3. Show one to three offers in order: a related bundle, then a single accessory, then a low-cost add-on.
4. Make the offer specific to this page, such as a discount not available elsewhere. If you say it expires, make it expire.
5. Show the order confirmation details above the offer.
6. Send a follow-up email one to two days later to customers who skipped the offer.

## What to measure

- **Take rate**: Share of orders where the customer accepts at least one post-purchase offer.
- **Incremental revenue per order**: Post-purchase revenue divided by all orders, including those that declined.
- **Return rate on added items**: Return rate for upsell items versus the main items.

## Best practices

- Frame it as a thank-you offer, not a hard sell.
- Use a discount that is real and not available elsewhere on the site. Customers check.
- Keep the whole flow short: one page, one to three offers.

## Common pitfalls

- Burying the order confirmation under offers, so customers are unsure their order went through.
- Offering something unrelated or something they just bought. After a starter kit, offer the refill.
- Ignoring returns. If added items come back often, the revenue is not real.
