# Trust Badges & Density

Trust badges are visual signals that reduce buyer worry: security seals, payment logos, star ratings, guarantees, press logos, and certifications. Having them is not enough. They need to sit where the worry happens: next to the buy button, at payment, and on the first screen of landing pages.

## How it works

Every online purchase carries questions. Is this site real? Is my card safe? Will the product match the photos? Can I return it? Each badge should answer one of these questions at the moment it comes up. A security seal in the footer does little. The same seal next to the pay button is seen at the moment of doubt.

## How to do it

1. List your current trust signals in four groups:
   - Security: SSL, payment provider marks, PCI compliance.
   - Social proof: star ratings, review counts, testimonials.
   - Authority: press logos, awards, certifications.
   - Risk reversal: guarantees, free returns, warranty.
2. Place security marks next to the card fields and the "Complete purchase" button.
3. Place star ratings and review counts near the price and CTA on product pages.
4. Add a single row of press or client logos below the hero on landing pages.
5. Show the return policy or guarantee near the CTA.
6. A/B test placement, number, and style of badges.

## What to measure

- **Conversion lift by placement**: Change in conversion rate from adding badges at a specific spot, measured with an A/B test.
- **Checkout abandonment rate**: Share of visitors who reach checkout but do not pay.
- **New visitor conversion rate**: Conversion rate for first-time visitors, who rely on trust signals the most.

## Best practices

- Pick the signals your audience cares about. A new consumer brand needs reviews and a guarantee. Enterprise software needs security certifications and client logos.
- Use clickable badges that link to a verification page when the issuer offers one.
- Put badges close to decision points and keep the rest of the page light.

## Common pitfalls

- Showing badges you have not earned. It is dishonest, can be illegal, and customers do check.
- Showing so many badges that the page looks cluttered and loads slower. Pick the four to six that matter most.
- Forgetting mobile. Make sure key badges, especially at checkout, are visible without scrolling on a phone.
