# Street Teams & Sampling

A street team is a group of people who meet potential customers face to face. They hand out samples, run demos, and collect contact details in busy places. For food, drink, consumer goods, and local services, a sample in hand often gets more attention than an ad.

## How it works

A good sampling interaction combines a free gift, a friendly person, a visible crowd, and a chance to try the product. Once someone tastes or holds a product, it feels more familiar. Placement decides results. A protein bar sample at a gym exit will do far better than the same sample on a random corner.

## How to do it

1. Define the interaction: give a sample, run a demo, collect an email, or book an appointment. Keep it natural and short.
2. Pick places where your customers already gather: gyms, farmers markets, campuses, transit hubs, festivals, or outside related stores (with permission).
3. Hire and train ambassadors who fit the brand. Teach them the product so they can talk, not recite.
4. Capture every lead. Use a tablet, a QR code to a form, or a text-to-enter contest to get at least a name and email.
5. Log samples, leads, and sales by location, time slot, and ambassador.

## What to measure

- **Cost per lead and cost per customer**: total program cost (product, labor, logistics) divided by leads and by customers.
- **Sample-to-purchase rate**: buyers who used the event code or URL divided by people sampled.
- **Interactions per hour**: conversations or samples per ambassador per hour, by location and time.

## Best practices

- Test several locations and time slots in week one, then move hours to the best ones.
- Give ambassadors room to have real conversations.
- Hand out an offer people can use right away, for example "Show this card for 20% off today."

## Common pitfalls

- Running a team with no lead capture, so nothing can be measured.
- Choosing locations by foot traffic instead of audience fit.
- Staffing with people who do not fit the brand or do not care. One bad interaction undoes many good ones.
