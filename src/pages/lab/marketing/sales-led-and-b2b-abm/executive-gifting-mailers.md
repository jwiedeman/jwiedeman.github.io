# Executive Gifting & Mailers

Executive gifting sends physical items, such as a gift, a handwritten note, or a custom package, to senior decision-makers who do not respond to email. Executives get far more email than physical mail, so a thoughtful package is more likely to be noticed. The gift opens the door. The follow-up books the meeting.

## How it works

A thoughtful gift creates a sense of reciprocity. The point is not the dollar value. It is showing that you spent real time understanding the person. A book tied to their published interests, or a note that references a talk they gave, signals effort that an email template cannot.

## How to do it

1. **Choose a short list.** Gifting is expensive per touch. Limit it to high-value accounts and senior contacts who have not answered digital outreach. Keep each campaign small, for example 25-50 people.
2. **Research each recipient.** Review their interests, public talks, LinkedIn posts, and company initiatives. Pick a gift that clearly relates to something they care about.
3. **Design the package.** Include quality packaging, a handwritten note, the gift, and one clear next step: a personal URL, a QR code to a short video, or a booking link. Write the CTA as an invitation.
4. **Coordinate with digital follow-up.** Send an email a few days after delivery that references the package.
5. **Track delivery and results.** Use tracked shipping. Log each send in your CRM. Start the follow-up sequence from the confirmed delivery date, not the send date.

## What to measure

- **Response rate**: replies, LinkedIn responses, or meetings, divided by confirmed deliveries.
- **Cost per meeting**: total campaign cost (gifts, shipping, creative), divided by meetings booked.
- **Pipeline influenced**: pipeline from gifted accounts within a 90-day window.

## Best practices

- Pair every gift with a handwritten note that shows real research. "I read your piece on [topic] and thought of this" beats "We would love to connect."
- Send something useful or consumable, not branded swag. A relevant book, quality food, or a donation in their name works better than a logo water bottle.
- Make responding easy. A booking link or "reply to this note" fits the personal tone better than a form.
- Use a gifting platform such as Sendoso or Reachdesk to manage inventory, shipping, and CRM logging.

## Common pitfalls

- Sending something that feels like a bribe. An expensive gift card to a stranger feels transactional. Aim for relevance, not value.
- Skipping follow-up. Without a coordinated email and call sequence, most gifts produce a moment of thanks and nothing else.
- Ignoring gift policies. Many large companies cap the value of gifts employees can accept. Check the policy, or send low-value, thoughtful items.
