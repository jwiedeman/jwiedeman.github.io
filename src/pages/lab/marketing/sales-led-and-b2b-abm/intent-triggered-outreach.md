# Intent-Triggered Outreach

Intent-triggered outreach uses signals that a company is researching your category to decide when to contact it. Instead of calling accounts that may have no need, you contact the ones that appear to be in-market now. The message arrives while the prospect is already thinking about the problem.

## How it works

Intent data comes from three sources. First-party data is activity on your own site and product. Second-party data is activity on review sites such as G2 or TrustRadius. Third-party data comes from providers such as Bombora or 6sense that track content consumption across the web. A rise in activity on topics related to your product suggests a buying process has started. Reaching the account early in that window gives you a head start.

## How to do it

1. **Choose data sources.** Start with first-party website visitor identification and one third-party provider. Add review-site intent if buyers in your category use those sites.
2. **Set thresholds.** Define what counts as a real signal. Example: three or more visits to pricing or product pages from one company within 7 days, or a high buying-stage score from your provider.
3. **Route alerts to reps.** Connect intent alerts to your CRM and sales engagement tool. When an account crosses the threshold, create a task for the owner with context: topics, pages viewed, and likely contacts.
4. **Write intent-specific sequences.** Reference the broader topic, not the tracking. Do not say "I saw you on our pricing page." Say "Many [industry] teams are reviewing [category] tools right now. Here is how our customers approach it."
5. **Compare against normal outbound.** Track meetings, opportunities, and wins from intent-triggered accounts versus other outbound. Use the results to adjust thresholds and judge each data source.

## What to measure

- **Intent-to-meeting rate**: intent-triggered accounts that book a qualified meeting within 30 days, divided by intent-triggered accounts.
- **Speed to contact**: average time from signal to the rep's first touch.
- **Intent source ROI**: pipeline and revenue from intent-triggered outreach, divided by the cost of the data provider.

## Best practices

- Require both fit and intent. An in-market account that does not match your ICP is still a poor prospect.
- Match urgency to signal strength. A pricing page visit deserves a same-day response. A broad topic surge can be worked within the week.
- Give reps the context, not just the alert. Knowing which topic spiked lets them tailor the message.
- Use intent in paid media. Increase spend on accounts showing intent and reduce it on inactive ones.

## Common pitfalls

- Mentioning the data itself. "I saw you have intent signals" damages credibility. Let the data shape the message, not become it.
- Treating all signals the same. A broad topic surge is weaker than a visit to a competitor comparison page. Weight effort accordingly.
- Trusting third-party intent alone. If an account shows high third-party intent but has never visited your site, the signal may be noise. Look for first-party confirmation before investing heavy effort.
