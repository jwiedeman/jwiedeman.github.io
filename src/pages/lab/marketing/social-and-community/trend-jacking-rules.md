# Trend-Jacking Rules

Trend-jacking means joining a trending conversation, sound, meme, or cultural moment to borrow its attention. Done well, the brand seems current and human. Done badly, it looks out of touch or exploitative. The difference comes down to rules: which trends to join, how fast to move, and where participation turns into appropriation.

## How it works

Content that uses a trending sound, format, or topic often gets wider distribution than it would on its own, because platforms want trends to keep people engaged. Your job is to pair the trend with your brand in a way that adds something to the conversation rather than just riding it.

## How to do it

1. Assign one person to check trends daily on your main platforms: TikTok's Creative Center and trending sounds, Trending on X, trending audio in Instagram Reels, plus Reddit and Google Trends for wider culture. Most trends stay relevant for only a few days.
2. Run the relevance check. Can the brand credibly join? Does our audience care? Can we add something specific or funny? If any answer is no, skip it.
3. Run the sensitivity check. Is the trend tied to a tragedy, political fight, or social justice issue? If yes, stay out. Also skip trends already crowded with brand posts.
4. Use a fast approval path for trend content: the social team drafts, one named approver signs off within about two hours, then publish.
5. Aim to publish the same day you spot the trend.
6. Log each trend post's results against your average post. Record which trend types, platforms, and team members work best, and write that into a playbook.

## What to measure

- **Engagement vs. baseline**: compare likes, comments, shares, and saves on trend posts with your average organic post.
- **Time to publish**: log hours from spotting the trend to posting.
- **Comment sentiment**: label comments on trend posts as positive, neutral, or negative, and watch for words like "cringe."

## Best practices

- Pick trends where your niche adds a specific angle. A security company explaining a trend with hacking metaphors works better than the same company doing a generic dance.
- Keep a trend kit: templates, voice guidelines, and pre-approved joke formats.
- Limit trend posts to one or two a week. Joining every trend dilutes the brand.

## Common pitfalls

- Joining trends linked to tragedy or social issues. Never trend-jack death, disasters, political movements, or social justice causes.
- Moving too slowly. If approval takes days, fix the workflow before trying this tactic.
- Forcing a connection. If you have to explain how the trend relates to your product, skip it.
