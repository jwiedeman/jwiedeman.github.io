# Positioning A/B Tests

Positioning usually lives in slide decks and is settled by internal agreement. A positioning test puts different framings of your value in front of real prospects and measures which one gets more clicks, signups, or sales. It turns a high-stakes judgment call into an experiment.

## How it works

Every positioning statement is a bet that one framing of your value matters more to buyers than the alternatives. A test makes that bet measurable. Show different audiences different angles through ad headlines, landing page hero copy, or social posts, and compare what they do next. You are not testing wording. You are testing the strategic claim behind it.

## How to do it

1. Write 3-5 angles that are truly different, not rewordings. For a project management tool: speed ("Ship projects faster"), reliability ("Never miss a deadline"), visibility ("See every project in one view"), and alignment ("Keep the team in sync without meetings").
2. Pick a fast, low-commitment surface. Paid search or social ad headlines work well. Landing page hero sections also work with a split-testing tool such as Optimizely or VWO. Do not rely on email subject lines alone; open rates are noisy.
3. Keep everything else equal. Use the same targeting, budget, design, and timing for each variant. For landing pages, split traffic randomly.
4. Set the sample size before you start with a significance calculator, and do not stop early.
5. Read the whole funnel, not just the first metric. A headline can win on clicks and lose on paid conversion. Compare CTR, signup rate, activation, and early retention.

## What to measure

- **CTR per variant**: clicks divided by impressions for each angle.
- **Conversion rate per variant**: signups, demo requests, or purchases divided by visitors for each angle.
- **CPA per variant**: spend divided by conversions for each angle in paid channels.

## Best practices

- Test angles, not copy. "Ship faster" vs. "Deliver faster" is a copy test. "Ship faster" vs. "Never lose track of a task" is a positioning test.
- Test before a rebrand, site overhaul, or major campaign. A small ad budget can prevent a large campaign built on the wrong angle.
- Test each customer segment separately. Startups and enterprises may respond to different angles, and a blended result hides that.

## Common pitfalls

- Calling a winner too early. Small samples produce noise. Commit to the sample size up front.
- Assuming one surface speaks for all. An angle that wins in ads may not win on a landing page or in a sales deck. Confirm on at least two surfaces.
- Letting the test override reality. If "fastest" wins but your product is not the fastest, you will win clicks and then lose customers. Treat results as strong input, not the only input.
