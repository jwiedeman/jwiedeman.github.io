# Double-Sided Rewards

A double-sided referral program rewards two people: the customer who refers (the advocate) and the friend who signs up or buys. Well-known examples include Dropbox (extra storage for both), PayPal (cash for both), and Uber (a free ride for both). The referral becomes a deal for the friend, not a favor for the advocate.

## How it works

When only the referrer gets a reward, sharing can feel self-serving. When the friend also gets something, the advocate has an easy reason to share: "I'm giving you a deal." This tends to raise both how often people share and how often friends accept.

## How to do it

1. Set the reward budget from your unit economics. The combined cost of both rewards should be lower than your paid-channel customer acquisition cost (CAC). Common structures: matching credit (give $20, get $20), matching product (a free month each), or mixed (15% off for the friend, $10 credit for the advocate).
2. Pick the reward trigger. Signup gives the most volume and the weakest referrals. First purchase is a balanced default. Renewal or sustained use gives the best quality and the lowest volume. Start with first purchase.
3. Build a short flow: a unique referral link, one-click sharing by email, SMS, and social, a landing page that states both rewards, and automatic payout. Remove every step you can between "share" and "reward."
4. Track the full chain from link click to account creation to qualifying action to payout. Use referral software (for example ReferralCandy, Friendbuy, or GrowSurf) or build it yourself. Test the tracking before launch.
5. Launch by emailing your most engaged customers first (high NPS, repeat buyers, active users) with a personal invitation to the program.

## What to measure

- **Referral rate** — successful referrals divided by active customers in the period.
- **Referral CAC vs. paid CAC** — total reward cost divided by referred customers acquired, compared with CAC from paid channels.
- **Referred customer LTV** — lifetime value of referred customers compared with customers from other channels.

## Best practices

- Consider giving the friend a slightly larger reward than the advocate. It makes the share feel generous. Test both versions.
- Show the referral prompt right after a good experience (delivery, a completed task, a milestone), not during onboarding.
- Test reward types. Some audiences prefer cash, others prefer credit or a donation made in their name.

## Common pitfalls

- Rewards too small to matter. A $5 reward on a $200 product will not move anyone. Size the reward to the price and the effort of sharing.
- No fraud controls. Use email verification, IP and device limits, and minimum purchase thresholds to stop fake accounts.
- Launching and forgetting. Review reward size, share flow, reminder emails, and fraud rules on a regular schedule.
