# Category Design & Reframe

Category design means creating a new market category, or reframing an existing one, so your company is seen as the one that defines it. Instead of competing on features inside someone else's category ("a better CRM"), you set a new frame where your strengths are the criteria that matter ("revenue intelligence").

## How it works

Buyers judge a product against the category they put it in. If you sit in an existing category, you inherit its evaluation criteria, which the incumbent usually set. Category design changes the frame so buyers use new criteria where your product wins. It needs three stories that agree with each other:

- **Company story:** why the world has changed.
- **Product story:** what the new solution looks like.
- **Category story:** why this category needs to exist now.

## How to do it

1. Name the problem the current category cannot solve, ideally one incumbents avoid because their business model depends on it. Back it with data, customer quotes, and market trends.
2. Name the category. Use two or three words that describe the outcome, not the technology. Test the names with at least 20 prospects and drop any that need explaining.
3. Write a two-page category blueprint: the problem, the category name, why now, what an ideal solution does (without naming your product), and the other players the category needs.
4. Launch the category in one coordinated push over four to six weeks: analyst briefings, press, a manifesto or report, an event, and product announcements.
5. Keep it going with ongoing research, community events, and third-party validation such as analyst reports and case studies. Track whether the name shows up in analyst coverage, job titles, and conference tracks over the next 12 to 18 months.

## What to measure

- **Category search volume:** track monthly searches for the category name in Google Trends or a keyword tool.
- **Share of category narrative:** count analyst reports, articles, and talks that name you as the category leader, divided by all mentions of the category.
- **Inbound language shift:** tag inbound leads and sales call notes by whether prospects describe the problem in your category language or the old one.

## Best practices

- Tie the launch to a real change in the market, such as a new regulation, technology, or buyer behavior. Without a believable "why now," the category reads as marketing.
- Let competitors into the category. A category with one company is a product pitch. When two or three others adopt the frame, buyers and analysts take it seriously, and you benefit as the one who defined it.
- Align the whole go-to-market with the category. Sales decks open with the category problem, job posts use the category name, and the website explains the category before the product.

## Common pitfalls

- Choosing a name only insiders understand. If a prospect cannot explain it to their boss in one sentence, it will not spread. "Conversational intelligence" works. "Asynchronous collaborative decision orchestration" does not.
- Launching before the product can deliver. Early reviewers will set the story, and it will be negative.
- Treating it as a one-time PR stunt. Companies that own a category, such as Salesforce with cloud CRM or HubSpot with inbound marketing, spent years on content, events, and partners to make the frame stick.
