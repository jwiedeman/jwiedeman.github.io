# Strategic Narrative

A strategic narrative is a company-level story. It explains how the world is changing, why the old way no longer works, and why your company is well placed to help customers adapt. A positioning statement describes what you do. A strategic narrative describes what is happening and why it calls for a new approach. Done well, it gives sales decks, investor pitches, recruiting pages, and campaigns the same "why."

## How it works

The narrative has three parts:

1. **The shift:** a real change in the market or the world.
2. **The stakes:** what people who ignore the shift will lose.
3. **The better future:** what becomes possible with the right approach, which your product enables.

Do not lead with the product. Lead with the shift, get agreement, and let the product follow as the logical answer.

## How to do it

1. Find two or three real trends (technology, behavior, regulation, economics) that are changing the rules for your audience. Support each with third-party data, analyst reports, or customer interviews.
2. Write the old-versus-new contrast: "Companies used to do X. Because of [shift], that now leads to [consequence]. The winners will do Y."
3. Describe the better future for the customer without naming your product. Keep it specific and achievable, for example: "Marketing teams will know which dollar drove which outcome, across every channel."
4. Introduce your product last, as the way to reach that future. Tie each capability to a specific part of it.
5. Test the narrative in at least 10 sales calls and 5 investor or board conversations. Note where people agree, where they lean in, and where attention drops. Revise.

## What to measure

- **Narrative adoption:** the share of reps, executives, and marketers who can explain the narrative unprompted, checked by survey or call recording review.
- **Content engagement:** average time on narrative content (manifesto page, keynote video, deck) compared with product content.
- **Media pickup:** count of earned media mentions that use your framing of the shift.

## Best practices

- Keep it to one page or a three-minute talk. If you cannot sketch it on a whiteboard in five minutes, it is too complex.
- Refresh data and examples every year. Keep the structure stable.
- Make the customer the hero. They are smart for seeing the shift and willing to adapt. Your product is the tool that helps them.

## Common pitfalls

- Starting with "We built X because..." Nobody cares about the product until they feel the change.
- Picking a shift that is too obvious or too early. "Digital transformation is happening" says nothing. A shift that most of your audience will not face for years is too early. Pick one people recognize but have not solved.
- Promising a future the product cannot reach soon. Align the story with what you can deliver in the next 6 to 12 months, or it will drive churn.
