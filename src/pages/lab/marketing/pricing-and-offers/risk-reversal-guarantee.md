# Risk Reversal & Guarantees

Risk reversal moves the risk of a purchase from the buyer to the seller. Common forms are money-back guarantees, free trials, satisfaction guarantees, and performance warranties. A hesitant buyer is weighing possible benefit against the risk of wasting money. A clear guarantee makes the worst case a refund instead of a loss.

## How it works

Prospect theory finds that losses feel roughly twice as strong as equal gains. Spending $100 on something untested feels risky. A guarantee removes the loss, so the decision feels safe. Guarantee length should match how long the product takes to deliver value, so buyers have time to see results before deciding.

## How to do it

1. Choose a guarantee type:
   - Money-back: "Full refund within 60 days, no questions asked."
   - Performance: "If you do not see X within 90 days, we refund you." Use only when the result is measurable.
   - Satisfaction: "If you are not satisfied, we will make it right."
2. Set the length to cover your time-to-value. Fast-value products can use 30 days. Products that take weeks to show results need 60-90 days.
3. Show the guarantee on the pricing page, product pages, checkout, and in emails.
4. Make refunds easy: one click or one email. Do not require calls or written reasons.
5. Log the reason for every refund and share the patterns with product and marketing teams.

## What to measure

- **Conversion lift**: conversion rate with the guarantee shown minus the rate without it, from an A/B test.
- **Refund rate**: refunds divided by purchases in the same period.
- **Net revenue impact**: added revenue from the conversion lift minus refunds paid.

## Best practices

- Feature the guarantee when competitors do not offer one.
- Offer a stronger guarantee for higher-priced plans and for first-time buyers.
- Pair the guarantee with onboarding help so customers reach results and rarely need to claim it.

## Common pitfalls

- Hiding the guarantee in the terms of service. If buyers do not see it, it does nothing.
- Adding so many conditions that it stops being a guarantee. Keep conditions few and reasonable.
- Using a guarantee to cover a weak product. A high refund rate points to a product or messaging problem. Fix that first.
