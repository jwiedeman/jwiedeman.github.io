# Native Lead Forms

Native lead forms let people submit their details inside the ad platform. They never visit your website. The form is pre-filled from their profile, so submitting takes a tap or two.

These forms usually bring more leads at a lower cost per lead than landing pages. Lead quality is often lower, so fast follow-up and qualification decide whether they pay off.

## How it works

Every step between an ad and a form loses people: slow pages, distractions, and typing. A native form removes those steps. On Meta, tapping "Sign Up" opens an Instant Form with name, email, and phone already filled in.

The tradeoff is intent. Someone who fills out a landing page form chose to do the work. Someone who taps a pre-filled button may not remember doing it. Qualifying questions, speed to contact, and sending quality data back to the platform make up the difference.

## How to do it

1. Pick the platform that fits your audience: Meta Instant Forms for broad B2C and B2B reach, LinkedIn Lead Gen Forms for job title and company data, TikTok lead generation for younger audiences, and Google lead form assets on Search and YouTube.
2. Keep forms short. Use 3-5 fields for consumer offers and 5-7 for B2B (add company, job title, company size).
3. Add 1-2 qualifying questions that require a choice, such as "What is your monthly marketing budget?" with ranges.
4. Write the intro screen to say what they will get, when, and what happens next.
5. Put a next step on the thank-you screen: a website link, a download, or a booking link.
6. Connect the form to your CRM through the platform's native integration or a tool like Zapier.
7. Send an instant alert to sales by Slack, email, or SMS, and trigger an automated email or SMS within minutes of submission.
8. Score leads using their answers, company size, and source campaign.
9. Send qualified-lead and sale events back to the platform (for example, Meta Conversions API or LinkedIn offline conversions) so it optimizes for quality, not just form fills.

## What to measure

- **CPL vs. landing page CPL**: cost per lead for native forms compared to landing page forms with the same audience and creative.
- **Lead-to-qualified rate**: share of form submissions that become marketing or sales qualified leads.
- **Cost per qualified lead**: spend divided by qualified leads, not raw leads.
- **Speed to first contact**: time from submission to first outreach.

## Best practices

- Use Meta's "Higher intent" form type. It adds a review step before submit and cuts accidental submissions.
- Match form fields to your CRM's required fields so leads do not get stuck in the integration.
- A/B test the number of qualifying questions. Fewer leads can still mean more qualified pipeline.

## Common pitfalls

- Judging campaigns on CPL alone. Example: $3 leads that qualify at 2% cost more per qualified lead than $8 leads that qualify at 15%.
- Following up slowly. Native form leads have lower intent and go cold fast.
- Not sending lead quality back to the platform. Without that signal, it optimizes for people who submit forms but never buy.
