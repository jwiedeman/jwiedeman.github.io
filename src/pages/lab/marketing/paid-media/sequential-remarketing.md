# Sequential Remarketing

Sequential remarketing shows past visitors a series of ads in a planned order. Each ad adds something new: first objections, then proof, then an offer. This avoids showing the same "you forgot something" ad for a month.

## How it works

Standard remarketing shows every visitor the same ad until they buy or drop out of the audience. But a visitor's mindset changes. In the first few days they are still deciding. After a week they may have forgotten. After two weeks they need a new reason to come back.

Sequential remarketing splits visitors by how long ago they visited and how deep they went. Each group gets creative written for that stage.

## How to do it

1. Create audiences by time since visit, for example 0-3 days, 4-7 days, 8-14 days, and 15-30 days.
2. Exclude each shorter window from the longer ones so a person is in only one group at a time.
3. Build these on Meta with website custom audiences by day range, and on Google with audience segments by membership duration.
4. Write creative for each window. Days 0-3: restate the value and answer the top objections (guarantee, free shipping). Days 4-7: add social proof (reviews, testimonials). Days 8-14: offer something low-risk (guide, demo, comparison). Days 15-30: make a direct offer with a deadline.
5. Split audiences by depth too: blog readers, product page viewers, and cart abandoners.
6. Give cart abandoners the shortest, most direct sequence. Give blog readers more education first.
7. Set a frequency cap for each window, for example 3-5 impressions per person.
8. Exclude buyers from all remarketing as soon as they convert.
9. Move people who finish the sequence without buying into long-term nurture, or suppress them from paid.

## What to measure

- **Sequence completion rate**: share of people who were served every stage of the sequence.
- **Conversion rate by stage**: conversions attributed to each stage's ads divided by people reached in that stage.
- **Frequency to conversion**: average impressions a buyer saw before converting.

## Best practices

- Put your strongest objection-handling ads in the first 72 hours, when intent is highest.
- Change formats between stages: carousel early, video testimonials in the proof stage, simple static offers at the end.
- Line up remarketing with your email sequence so the offers do not conflict.
- Match the sequence length to your real buying window.

## Common pitfalls

- Making the sequence longer than your sales cycle. If most people buy within 7 days, a 30-day sequence wastes the last three weeks.
- Using the same look for every stage. People stop seeing ads that look familiar.
- Showing "come back" ads to people who already bought. Check that buyer exclusions update quickly.
