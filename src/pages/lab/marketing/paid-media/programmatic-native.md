# Programmatic Native Advertising

Programmatic native ads look like the content around them. They appear as "recommended articles" or in-feed sponsored posts on news sites, blogs, and niche publications. You buy them through networks like Taboola and Outbrain, or through a DSP.

## How it works

A banner interrupts reading. A native ad sits in the flow of it. The ad takes on the publisher's fonts and layout, so it reads like a content recommendation.

Programmatic buying adds targeting and bidding. You can target by interest, context, or past visits across many publishers at once, and bid on each impression. This makes native a good fit for mid-funnel content, when people are in a reading mindset.

## How to do it

1. Pick a channel. Taboola and Outbrain run large discovery networks. You can also buy native formats through a DSP such as The Trade Desk or DV360.
2. Write headlines that read like article titles. "How one team cut onboarding time in half" works better here than "Buy our HR software."
3. Use real, editorial-style images. Skip stock photos with text overlays.
4. Prepare 5-10 headline and image pairs to test.
5. Start with contextual or interest targeting that matches your product category.
6. Add retargeting for people who clicked a native ad but did not convert.
7. For B2B, use the third-party data segments your DSP offers, such as company size or job function.
8. Send clicks to a content page, not a product page. Use a blog post or advertorial that delivers on the headline.
9. Place several conversion points in the content: email signup, demo request, or free trial.
10. Track post-click actions (time on page, scroll depth, signups) and optimize toward them instead of clicks.
11. Review placement reports and block publishers that drive clicks but no engagement.

## What to measure

- **CTR by publisher**: clicks divided by impressions for each publisher site.
- **Post-click engagement rate**: share of visitors who pass your engagement bar, such as 30 seconds on page or a scroll past 50%.
- **Cost per engaged visit**: spend divided by visitors who met that engagement bar.

## Best practices

- Write headlines as story pitches. "How," "Why," and numbered lists fit the editorial setting.
- Prune low-quality publishers early, within the first couple of weeks.
- Use your best-performing blog posts as landing pages before you pay for custom advertorials.

## Common pitfalls

- Sending native traffic to a product or checkout page. People clicked to read, not to buy.
- Looking only at campaign totals. Publisher quality varies widely, so check placements weekly.
- Using clickbait headlines. They get clicks but break trust when the page does not match.
