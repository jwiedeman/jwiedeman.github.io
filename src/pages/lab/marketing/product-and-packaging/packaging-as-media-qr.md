# Packaging as Media & QR Codes

Every product you ship comes in packaging, and every buyer sees it at unboxing and first use. Packaging as media treats the box, label, insert, or wrap as a communication channel. QR codes, short URLs, and printed content connect the product to digital destinations: tutorials, registration, reviews, refill subscriptions, loyalty programs, and community. You already pay for this surface, so using it well is a cheap way to reach customers.

## How it works

Most phone cameras now read QR codes without a separate app, and people are used to scanning them for menus, tickets, and payments. That makes a QR code on packaging a reliable link to a web page. The person scanning is already holding your product and has chosen to act, so their attention is higher than typical web traffic.

## How to do it

1. Pick one destination per product, matched to what the customer needs at that moment: a setup video, a usage guide or recipes, warranty or loyalty registration, a review request, or a refill page. One product, one call to action, one destination.
2. Place the code where it is easy to see and pair it with a clear reason to scan: "Scan for setup video," "Scan to join rewards."
3. Build a mobile-first landing page. Make it load fast and deliver the promised content immediately: no popups, no app download, no login wall.
4. Use dynamic QR codes that point to a redirect you control, so you can change the destination without reprinting.
5. Add tracking per product line, batch, or retail channel (unique codes or UTM parameters).
6. Plan the next step after the scan: tutorial leads to email signup, warranty registration leads to loyalty, review leads to a refill offer.

## What to measure

- **QR scan rate**: scans divided by units sold, per product and per code version.
- **Post-scan conversion rate**: percent of scanners who complete the target action (register, subscribe, review, reorder).
- **Contact capture rate**: percent of scanners who give an email or create an account.

## Best practices

- Use dynamic codes. They let you update destinations, A/B test landing pages, and track scans.
- Test placement and wording on a small print run first. Compare scan rates between versions, for example front versus back, or "Scan for tips" versus "Scan to activate warranty."
- Add an insert card when the package has little print space. It can carry the QR code, a short note, and a reorder offer.

## Common pitfalls

- Sending scanners to a generic homepage. The page must match exactly what the packaging promised.
- Codes printed too small or in low contrast. Use a dark code on a light background and test physical prints in different lighting before production.
- Treating it as a one-time project. Review scan data, update destinations, and test new calls to action on a regular schedule.
