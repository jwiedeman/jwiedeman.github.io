# Intent Mining from Search & Forums

Intent mining means reading search queries, forum threads, reviews, and Q&A sites to learn what buyers are looking for and struggling with. People write these without your company in the room, so the language is unfiltered. Use it for content, ad copy, and positioning.

## How it works

Each query or post signals a stage in the buying journey. "What is [category]" is early research. "Best [category] for [use case]" is active evaluation. "[Your product] vs [competitor]" is a decision in progress. "[Your product] cancel" is churn risk. Collect and tag these signals and you get a map of demand, competitors, and messaging gaps.

## How to do it

1. List your sources. For search: Google Search Console, a keyword tool (Ahrefs, Semrush, or Google Keyword Planner), and Google's "People also ask" and autocomplete. For communities: relevant subreddits, Quora, Stack Exchange, industry forums, G2 and Capterra reviews, X threads, and LinkedIn comments.
2. Build a seed list of 20 to 30 terms: your product, competitors, category terms, problem descriptions, and use cases. Export related keywords and collect 50 to 100 forum threads about your category.
3. Tag each signal by stage (awareness, consideration, decision, retention) and type (informational, navigational, commercial, transactional). Note the specific need, objection, or comparison. Keep it in a spreadsheet.
4. Find the gaps: topics with high search or discussion volume where you have no content or presence. Rank them by volume and closeness to purchase.
5. Put the language to work. Use exact search phrases in titles and ad headlines. Turn forum objections into FAQ answers and sales talking points. Use comparison criteria to structure feature pages. Repeat the analysis each quarter.

## What to measure

- **Intent coverage rate**: share of high-intent queries where you rank in the top 10 or run a targeted ad.
- **Mention sentiment ratio**: positive to negative mentions across reviews and forums, tracked monthly.
- **Query-to-content gaps**: count of high-intent queries with meaningful volume and no dedicated page on your site.

## Best practices

- Prioritize "vs" and "alternative" queries. These buyers are close to a decision. An honest comparison page lets you frame the choice.
- Read competitor reviews for repeated complaints. If many mention a steep learning curve, that is an angle you can own. Reuse their exact phrases.
- Set up ongoing alerts for your brand, competitors, and category terms. This works best as a continuous habit, not a one-time audit.

## Common pitfalls

- Tracking only branded queries. Brand searches show existing awareness. Category and problem queries show the larger market that does not know you yet.
- Taking forum posts as representative. Heavy forum users are not your whole market. Use forums to find language and themes, then confirm with surveys or search volume.
- Producing a huge spreadsheet nobody uses. Limit output to the top 10 findings per quarter, each with an owner and a deadline.
