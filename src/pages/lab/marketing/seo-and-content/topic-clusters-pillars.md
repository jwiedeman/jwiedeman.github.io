# Topic Clusters & Pillar Pages

A topic cluster groups content around one core theme. A pillar page covers the broad topic. Cluster pages each cover one subtopic in depth and link back to the pillar. This structure shows search engines and readers that your site covers the subject thoroughly, and the links help each page get found.

## How it works

Search engines understand topics and how they relate, not just individual keywords. A single post on "email marketing tips" competes alone. A pillar on email marketing, linked to pages on segmentation, deliverability, automation, subject lines, testing, and compliance, shows depth across the whole subject. Links between the pillar and its cluster pages pass authority and help crawlers find every page.

## How to do it

1. Choose three to five broad topics tied directly to your product, each large enough to support many subtopics.
2. For each topic, list the questions and subtopics your audience searches for. Use "People Also Ask," keyword tools, and competitor content to find gaps.
3. Group the list so each subtopic gets one dedicated page and no two pages target the same query.
4. Write the pillar page. Cover the topic at a high level, with one section per major subtopic and a table of contents.
5. Link from each pillar section to the matching cluster page.
6. Write each cluster page around one specific subtopic. Link back to the pillar with descriptive anchor text, and link to related cluster pages where it helps the reader.
7. Publish the pillar first, then add cluster pages and update the pillar's links as each one goes live.
8. Review every page after six months. Refresh, retarget, or merge pages that underperform, and add new subtopics as they appear.

## What to measure

- **Cluster organic traffic**: total organic sessions across the pillar and all its cluster pages, from a content-group filter in analytics.
- **Cluster rankings**: number of cluster keywords ranking in the top 10, from a rank tracker.
- **Internal link clicks**: clicks between pillar and cluster pages, from link-click events in analytics.

## Best practices

- Make the pillar the single page you would recommend to someone new to the topic.
- Use descriptive anchor text that names the topic when linking clusters to the pillar.
- Update the pillar each quarter with new sections and links to new cluster pages.

## Common pitfalls

- Picking a pillar topic that is too broad. "Marketing" is too wide; "content marketing for SaaS" is focused enough.
- Cannibalization. Two pages targeting the same query compete with each other, so merge or retarget them.
- Publishing cluster pages with no pillar to link to. Launch the pillar first, even if it is short at the start.
