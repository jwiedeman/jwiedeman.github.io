# Content Refresh Cycles

A content refresh cycle is a regular process for finding pages that have lost traffic or gone out of date and improving them. Most sites have older posts that once ranked well and have since slipped. Updating an existing page is often less work than writing a new one, and the page keeps the links and history it has already earned.

## How it works

Search results change over time. Competitors publish newer, more complete pages. Facts, prices, and screenshots go stale. Search intent can shift, so the type of page Google shows for a query changes. A refresh fixes these gaps at the same URL. The page keeps its backlinks and history while becoming current and competitive again.

## How to do it

1. Export the last 12 months of page data from Google Search Console. Flag pages whose clicks or impressions have dropped 20% or more from their peak.
2. Rank the flagged pages by peak traffic, business value (do they drive sign-ups or sales?), and how much work a refresh needs.
3. For each page, search its main keyword and compare your page to the current top results. Note the cause of the decline: stale facts, missing subtopics, a shift in intent, or technical problems such as broken images or slow loading.
4. Fix the cause. Update facts, examples, and screenshots. Add sections that competing pages cover and yours does not. If intent has shifted, restructure the page to match what now ranks.
5. Improve the basics on every refresh: the introduction, headings, internal links, title tag, and meta description.
6. Keep the same URL. Update the "last modified" date only when you made real changes.
7. Request re-indexing with the URL Inspection tool in Search Console.
8. Repeat every quarter. Pick the top 10-20 candidates each cycle and check each refreshed page at 30, 60, and 90 days.

## What to measure

- **Traffic recovery rate**: clicks in the 90 days after a refresh, divided by the page's peak 90-day clicks.
- **Refresh return vs. new content**: organic clicks gained per hour of work, compared between refreshes and new pages.
- **Content decay rate**: share of indexed pages down 20% or more from their peak, checked each quarter.

## Best practices

- Start with pages that drive revenue, such as comparison or product pages, before low-traffic informational posts.
- Make real changes. Changing only the date does not make a page better and will not hold rankings.
- Merge thin or overlapping posts on the same subtopic into one stronger page and 301-redirect the old URLs to it.

## Common pitfalls

- Changing the URL without a strong reason. Redirects work, but keeping the URL is simpler and safer.
- Refreshing pages that cannot realistically rank. Focus on queries where your site can compete for page one.
- Breaking internal links. Adding and removing sections can orphan links, so check inbound and outbound internal links on every refresh.
