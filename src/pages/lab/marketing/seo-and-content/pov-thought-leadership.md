# POV Thought Leadership

POV (point of view) thought leadership is content that takes a clear position instead of repeating common advice. It challenges an industry assumption, proposes a new framework, or argues an unpopular view with evidence. It stands out because readers have not seen it before, and it is more likely to be shared, cited, and remembered.

## How it works

Most industry content repeats the same advice in slightly different words. A clear position, based on what your company actually knows, is easier to remember and gives people something to discuss. Taking a stand also signals confidence and expertise, which matters to experienced buyers. The position must be defensible. Disagreeing without evidence is noise; disagreeing with evidence is useful.

## How to do it

1. Write down what your company knows that others do not. Sources include your own data, a large body of hands-on work, or team members who are former practitioners in the field.
2. Read the top-ranking content on your key topics. List the assumptions everyone repeats.
3. Pick assumptions where your data or experience points to a different answer.
4. Structure each piece in four parts: the common view, why it is incomplete or wrong, your alternative, and the evidence.
5. Support the argument with specific numbers, named examples, and a clear explanation of how you reached the conclusion.
6. Publish under a named person, such as a founder or practitioner, not a generic brand byline.
7. Share it through that person's social posts, newsletters, podcast interviews, and talks. Invite respectful debate.
8. Choose three to five core beliefs and publish several pieces on each over time, so your audience links your brand to a consistent view.

## What to measure

- **Linking domains per post**: new referring domains to POV posts, compared with standard posts, from a backlink tool.
- **Share rate**: shares divided by views, from social analytics and on-page share tracking.
- **Branded search trend**: monthly searches for your brand or author names, from Search Console or a keyword tool.

## Best practices

- Disagree with ideas, not people.
- State the strongest version of the opposing view before answering it.
- Name the author and their relevant experience on the page.

## Common pitfalls

- Taking a contrarian stance without evidence. Experienced readers will notice.
- Hedging every point with "it depends." If there is no clear position, there is no POV.
- Backing down at the first pushback. Respond with more evidence, or update the view openly if the evidence changes.
